INSIDE THE EVERYDAY

A refrigerator moves heat.

The compressor draws in low-pressure vapor and compresses it. The condenser releases heat to the kitchen, turning the refrigerant into liquid. A fixed capillary tube drops its pressure. Inside the cabinet, the evaporator absorbs heat as the refrigerant boils.

Try a small experiment

Add up to six warm grocery loads. Their temperatures and heat transfer are simulated separately from the cabinet. See inside reveals the new items, blue circulating air and orange heat-leak arrows. Larger compressor displacement increases piston stroke and refrigerant flow at the same motor RPM; insulation changes the thickness of heat-leak arrows.

The 24-hour challenge resets to 4 °C with warm milk and vegetables, standard controls and a closed door. Keep the cabinet at 3–5 °C for at least 95% of the time after a one-hour settling period, using at most 0.60 kWh. This is a game target, not an appliance rating or food-safety assessment. The challenge ends automatically. Restart to try another strategy; Reset returns to free exploration.

About this model

This is an educational single-compartment refrigerator. Representative properties and a simplified R600a-like pressure curve are used, not a refrigerant property solver or appliance rating. Cooling, electrical input and rejected heat satisfy Qout = Qin + W. The thermostat uses ±0.5 °C hysteresis.

Wall heat conduction uses a 3.2 m² surface, 50 mm insulation and fixed surface-film resistances. Material conductivities are illustrative dry values; moisture and construction change them. Cabinet thermal capacity is 18 kJ/K. Opening the door adds a sensible heat load; humidity, frost, defrost and detailed pressure equalization are omitted. Grocery loads use a separate lumped temperature and 2.5 W/K heat exchange per load.

The front-facing cooling loop is separated from the cabinet for teaching; the condenser normally sits behind the cabinet. The piston and fan run in visual slow motion. Door opening changes heat load; inspection modes do not. The adjustable electricity price is an example, not a current tariff. Pause freezes both physics and mechanical animation.
